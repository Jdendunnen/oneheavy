🇩🇪 HKSPK — Heimkehr

Released
11 Sep 2026
Label
Independent
Genre
Black / Death Metal
Origin
🇩🇪 Frankfurt (Oder), Germany
Length
25:21 · 8 Tracks

I have listened to this album more times than I can count. I sat with each track, let each one breathe and followed the story from the first orchestral keys of Prelude through to the final, devastating notes of Heimkehr itself. What HKSPK have built here deserves more than a surface reading, and I am not prepared to give it anything less.

Prelude opens proceedings with exactly the right restraint — orchestral tone keys setting a mood of quiet menace in the background, creating space for the onslaught to come. It is a brief and deliberate statement of intent. By the time Torture arrives it is already too late to brace yourself. Ferocious blast beats, shouting vocals that never let up for a single second — there is nothing left on the bone here, nothing to chew on. The track gets you pumped and keeps you there until the last note. It does not give you a moment to recover and does not care that you need one.

White Secretions II is where the album reveals it has more colours than the opening assault might suggest. Chug, gallop, gallop — an ascending chord pattern over rhythmic drive that borders on bliss, the low gutturals sitting perfectly in the mix and complementing every move the instrumentation makes beneath them. I found myself wanting more of it the moment it ended. Sonnenjäger — the sun hunter — follows without missing a step, maintaining the album's momentum and keeping the trend in high spirits. Deep tremolo riffing doing what only deep tremolo riffing can do — the kind of guitar work I genuinely never tire of, coating the track in the icy atmospheric colour the first half of this record wears so well. Kill the Weak then throws something unexpected into the mix: a genuine punk energy that rolls straight into smooth double kick transitions without losing the thread for even a moment. I was not prepared for it. I was very impressed when it came. The album's more violent first half closes here, and it closes with force.

Then the record turns inward. Rot in Despair opens on clean tone — a deliberate, unhurried shift in mood — before the deep guttural delivery arrives, smooth and controlled and all the more devastating for its restraint. The vocal tone throughout this track is outstanding, and what builds beneath it leads into one of the finest outros I have heard in a very long time. It is that rare moment on a record where everything the band set out to do crystallises into a single passage that justifies the entire journey to reach it. Well done, lads. Rain in the Face carries that weight forward with layered, harmonised guitar parts that deeply impressed me — uniquely so. The casual strum that accompanies the song's broader themes feels considered rather than accidental. The outro — a howling, screamed delivery moving into spoken word, the rasp sitting beautifully over a clean tone close — is wonderful. Delivered with genuine conviction, yet again.

Then Heimkehr itself. The title track. The homecoming that was never going to offer closure. I want to borrow the band's own words here, because they say it better than I can:

“A melody rang out — of unfathomable sound.”

Eine Melodie erklang — von unergründlichem Klang.

Yes. That is this album. That is exactly what this album is. I am fortunate enough to have a physical reaction to music — the kind that produces goosebumps when something lands exactly as it should. The way HKSPK have composed this final track gave me that reaction. I felt it. Chills. I am grateful to Jim and the rest of HKSPK for reaching out to OneHeavy and trusting me with this work of art ahead of its release. I hope this review does it justice. I genuinely hope I have the honour of reviewing the next one.

Your metal brethren in Adelaide — Josef Den Dunnen, OneHeavy.
